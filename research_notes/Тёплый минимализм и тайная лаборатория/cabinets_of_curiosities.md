# Cabinets of Curiosities and Secret-Lab Collections: Web and Game Examples

Research date: 2026-10-29. Method: 14 web searches. Most page fetches failed because the session's network allowlist blocked the hosts (geneve.ch, francke-halle.de, itch.io pages, kulturstiftung-des-bundes.de, web.stanford.edu). Findings for those sources come from search-result text only and are marked "snippet". Dates are given where the source shows one; otherwise "undated". Rarity ratings are my judgment from what the searches surfaced, not a survey.

## Which online projects present a collection as a cabinet of curiosities, with objects that open into stories?

### Takeaway
Few true web projects give each object its own story. The strongest candidates are institutional: the City of Geneva's Mirabilia portal (launched 2020), the Francke Foundations' Wunderkammer app (project from 2021, objects "speak" with their voices), and a museum-hosted openable Getty cabinet. Most "cabinet of curiosities" hits are physical exhibitions, VR works, or personal portfolio sites. No Awwwards or Codrops case study surfaced.

### Cited Findings
- Mirabilia (City of Geneva, launched 2020): an online platform that presents "extraordinary, easily-accessible stories" by pairing artworks, archive documents and scientific objects that would not normally appear together; built by eight municipal institutions. Visitors can browse by material, object type, country or institution, and can build and share their own virtual galleries. — [Geneva city dossier](https://www.geneve.ch/actualites/dossiers-information/culture-creatrice-valeurs/culture-innovation/mediation-culturelle-outils-numeriques) (snippet; 2020 launch per this page); [Geneva digital-inclusion page](https://www.geneve.ch/themes/numerique/numerique-inclusif/collections-ligne) (snippet). Rarity: Uncommon.
- Francke Foundations, Halle, "Discover the Wunderkammer with Suddenlife Gaming": a web app for children aged 6–12 and families, set in the Cabinet of Artefacts and Natural Curiosities, combined with analogue games. A crocodile character, Tayé, asks players to save "the voices of the objects, which have been telling the story of the world here for over 300 years." The project began in 2021 and was part of the federally funded "dive in" programme. A large sailing-ship model in the cabinet is called "Jenny" for now. — [Francke Foundations Krokoseum voices page](https://francke-halle.de/en/education/krokoseum/voices) (snippet); [Francke project page](https://bildung.francke-halle.de/en/development/facilities/krokoseum-childrens-creative-centre/discover-the-wunderkammer-with-suddenlife-gaming) (snippet). Rarity: Rare (objects with voices is the closest match to "each object has a story").
- Getty Museum Display Cabinet interactive: a virtual model of a four-sided 17th-century cabinet. Users open drawers and panels and read about its history, materials and imagery. The same interactive is on getty.edu and in gallery N102 beside the real object. An AR version lets users spin, tilt and open it. — [Getty teacher guide PDF](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/classroom_resources/tips_tools/downloads/cabinet_teacher.pdf) (snippet, undated); [Getty AR feature](https://www.getty.edu/art/exhibitions/north_pavilion/ar/index.html) (snippet, undated). Sources conflict on origin: one Getty page says Dutch, c. 1630 (Kabinettschrank); exhibition pages say Augsburg, Germany. Rarity: Uncommon (museum site, but drawer-by-drawer reading is unusual).
- "Eldritch Study" (University of York heritage jam): an interactive render of an early-1900s cabinet of curiosities that can be explored online. — [heritagejam.hosted.york.ac.uk](https://heritagejam.hosted.york.ac.uk/index.php/eldritch-study/) (snippet; undated; not fetched). Rarity: Rare.
- Göttweig Abbey, "Wunderkammer(21). Barock digital": a digital transformation project for the Wunderkammer commissioned by Abbot Gottfried Bessel (1714–1749), aiming at immersive experiences and reusable digital assets. — [FH St. Pölten research page](https://research.fhstp.ac.at/en/projects/goettwaig-wunderkammer-21-.-barock-digital) (snippet; undated). Rarity: Uncommon.
- Karlsruhe Natural History Museum: an online knowledge portal named "WUNDERKAMMER", funded under the Baden-Württemberg programme "Digitale Wege ins Museum II". — [SMNK press release PDF](https://www.smnk.de/fileadmin/page_content/pressemitteilungen/PM_Wunderkammer_Naturkundemuseum_Karlsruhe2022.pdf) (snippet; date 2022 taken from the filename; not fetched). Rarity: Uncommon.
- Online Wunderkammer resource catalogue: a project page at azt.kleio.com. — [azt.kleio.com](https://azt.kleio.com/2_arbeiten/23_wunderkammer-online-resource-catalogue) (snippet; undated). Search summaries attribute it to ETH Zurich (Günther Vogt, 3-year project); this attribution is unverified.
- Rijksmuseum Boerhaave platform: described as scroll-driven with "storycards", interactive object modules and expandable knowledge accordions, tagline "Every scroll feeds curiosity." — [Norday award page](https://norday.nl/en/award/rijksmuseum-boerhaave/) (snippet only; component list not confirmed on the museum site; undated). Rarity: Uncommon.
- Objectspace (New Zealand) archived exhibit "Cabinet of Curiosities": shows fabric swatches, photos, labels, buttons, cards and postcards as clues to a designer's inspiration. — [archive.objectspace.org.nz](https://archive.objectspace.org.nz/Exhibitions/Detail/Cabinet+Of+Curiosities.html) (snippet; undated). Rarity: Uncommon.
- Not confirmed: Gail Wight, "Cabinet of Curiosities" at Stanford (web.stanford.edu/~gailw/Wight_Projects/Cabinet.html). Search summaries describe seven musings on evolution and call it a 2001 CD-ROM. The "2001" and "CD-ROM" details appear only in a search summary, not in the page text I could read. Unverified; fetch blocked.
- Not confirmed: an interactive Berlin Museum of Natural History DVD (2011) that lets users navigate by scientific, historic, fictional or aesthetic criteria. Source: search summary only. Unverified.
- Not examined (URLs surfaced, content unknown): wishious.cargo.site "Map of curiosities"; jasoneppink.com "The Wonder Cabinet"; the Merz Akademie "Digital Wunderkammer" student project; the Egyptian Museum of Turin temporary web project (2024/2025, per search summary).
- Awwwards / Codrops: searches for "cabinet of curiosities" returned no Awwwards or Codrops page. The few web-design hits were a Framer portfolio (contra.com "Ben's Vault") with asset cards and scroll transitions. Low relevance.

### Inferences
- Most "cabinet" web projects use the metaphor for navigation (drawers, panels, scroll) rather than for per-object narrative. The per-object voice (Francke) and per-object juxtaposition (Mirabilia) are the models closest to "each artifact has its own story."
- A museum-hosted, openable 3D object (Getty) is a low-cost way to make one object the whole page: drawers become sections.

### Gaps
- No Awwwards or Codrops case study found; retry with specific techniques (for example "scroll-driven museum" or "WebGL cabinet") in a later pass.
- Blocked fetches: geneve.ch, francke-halle.de, kulturstiftung-des-bundes.de, web.stanford.edu. Their details rest on snippets.
- Most sources are undated; no item counts verified for Mirabilia or the Karlsruhe portal.

## Which games use a collection-and-discovery loop around objects with written notes?

### Takeaway
Four loops recur: (1) an object triggers a story (Edith Finch); (2) notes or documents are the clues (Test Subject 1601, Chimera Labs, Isla Sombra); (3) a ledger that the player fills by deduction (Obra Dinn); (4) a searchable archive that reveals a few items at a time (Her Story), with unlinked pages found by searching (Hypnospace Outlaw). Specimen-collection with a procedure document (Researcher) is the closest to a "secret lab" fiction.

### Cited Findings
- What Remains of Edith Finch (2017): each family memorial object triggers a vignette about how that person died. Each vignette has a different surreal or allegorical style, and objects reflect the person's traits (a Bible, an astronaut's helmet, a hunting rifle). About 13 vignettes per Press Start. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Remains_of_Edith_Finch); [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/?p=1176862); [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/what-remains-of-edith-finch-is-a-story-made-of-sto); [Escapist](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/?p=24047); [Press Start, 2017-05-01](https://press-start.com.au/reviews/pc-reviews/2017/05/01/remains-edith-finch-review-exploration-death-depth/). Rarity as a mechanic: Uncommon (well known, rare as a website pattern).
- Return of the Obra Dinn (2018): a ledger lists every crew member and passenger with occupation and face; the core task is matching faces to names and causes of death (58 of 60 needed to finish). A pocket watch lets the player see the moment of death on a corpse, and chapters are deliberately not in chronological order. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn); [StatelyPlay review, 2018-11-09](https://statelyplay.com/2018/11/09/return-of-the-obra-dinn-is-a-goddamned-miracle/). The "frozen moment" and ledger details come from search snippets of several reviews. Rarity: Uncommon.
- Her Story (2015): a search-only archive of police interview clips. Each search returns at most five clips in chronological order, so the player adds terms to see more. A tracker marks watched clips green and the last-viewed clip yellow. Later searches return up to 15. Reviews criticise the repetition of the loop. — [Quarter Disorder review, 2015-07-09](https://quarterdisorder.com/2015/07/09/review-her-story/); [App Unwrapper, 2015-06-22](https://www.appunwrapper.com/2015/06/22/her-story-spoiler-free-review/); [Killscreen essay](https://killscreen.com/listening-her-story-looking-right-questions) (snippet). Rarity: Uncommon.
- Hypnospace Outlaw (2019): the player browses fake 1990s web pages to find rule-breaking content. Many pages are linked from nowhere on the site and are reached through search, through the "Super Search" unlock, or through game files. The designer proposed framing extra page sets as Internet Archive "captures." — [TV Tropes trivia](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Trivia/HypnospaceOutlaw); [Filmstories](https://filmstories.co.uk/gaming/hypnospace-outlaw-the-indie-game-that-simulates-the-90s-web/); [IndieHellZone, 2019-03-18](https://indiehellzone.com/2019/03/18/hypnospace-outlaw). Predecessor build: [Lost Media Wiki, Hypnospace Enforcer (2014)](https://lostmediawiki.com/Hypnospace_Enforcer_partially_found_Jay_Tholen_build_of_game_(2014)). Rarity: Rare as a mechanic.
- Test Subject 1601 (itch.io): notes left by previous test subjects are clues and hint at secrets deeper in the lab; the player chooses between obeying the voice that issues tests and pursuing the truth. — [itch.io page](https://raideraik.itch.io/test-subject-1601) (snippet; undated; not fetched). Rarity: Uncommon.
- Researcher (pneumajams, itch.io, HTML5): an ARG-style game in which the player uses a drone to collect a quota of specimens and processes them according to a standard operating procedure document. Four specimens are needed to keep the job. — [itch.io page](https://pneumajams.itch.io/researcher) (snippet; undated; not fetched). Rarity: Rare.
- Isla Sombra (Steam; the store link is candidate evidence only, unverified): a research-facility story with no tutorials; the player collects handbooks and learns lab equipment by reading them. — [Steam store link](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4593300) (mapping to Isla Sombra unverified; undated). Rarity: Uncommon.
- The Secret of Chimera Labs (iOS, aircamp games): an in-game notebook records clues. — [ToucArcade thread](https://toucharcade.com/community/threads/the-secret-of-chimera-labs-by-aircamp-games.277389/) (snippet; undated). Rarity: Uncommon.
- Exit: The Game – The Secret Lab (board game, cooperative escape room): codes, puzzles and collected objects unlock progress. — [Dice Tower](https://dicetower.com/board-game/203417) (snippet). Mainstream; included for the object-unlock pattern only.
- Academic reference: "Collecting, Exhibiting, Visiting, and Performing in the Wunderkammer of Animal Crossing" (DiGRA). — [dl.digra.org](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/view/2757) (title only; not read).

### Inferences
- Trigger-by-object (Edith Finch) and ledger-by-deduction (Obra Dinn) are the strongest models for "each artifact has a story," because the reveal happens after the player interacts with the object.
- Hypnospace's unlinked pages and Her Story's limited search results show how an archive can stay partly hidden: sections exist but are not in the navigation.
- Notes that come from earlier subjects or procedure documents (Test Subject 1601, Researcher) give fiction a document-based voice that suits a site made of sections and documents.

### Gaps
- Mechanics were checked against review and snippet text, not by playing. Dates are missing for most itch.io and Steam sources.
- Isla Sombra and the Steam link are not tied to a verified page.

## How do these projects handle labels, catalogue cards, provenance text and "found" objects?

### Takeaway
Four label styles appear: a curator's label (Getty drawers, Historic Deerfield guidance), a catalogue card or ledger entry the player fills in (Obra Dinn, Objectspace's cards), a first-person voice (Francke's objects), and juxtaposition as provenance (Mirabilia). Found items often arrive as notes, handbooks or archived captures.

### Cited Findings
- Getty: each drawer and panel has text about history, materials and imagery. — [Getty teacher guide](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/classroom_resources/tips_tools/downloads/cabinet_teacher.pdf) (snippet).
- Historic Deerfield guide for physical cabinets: recommends a label on each object explaining what it is, with a box or display as the core element. — [Historic Deerfield PDF](https://historic-deerfield.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Curiosity-and-Wonder-Exploring-Collections.pdf) (snippet; undated). This is a museum-education guide, not a web project.
- Objectspace: labels, cards and postcards are used as clues to a designer's process. — [archive.objectspace.org.nz](https://archive.objectspace.org.nz/Exhibitions/Detail/Cabinet+Of+Curiosities.html) (snippet).
- Library card-catalogue as a display case: a San Diego library blog describes the card catalogue as a way to present "interesting objects that invoke curiosity." — [Pressman blog](https://fall2025-ecl596.jessicapressman.com/?p=2680) (snippet; student blog, low authority).
- Mirabilia uses juxtaposition in place of a provenance label: it pairs works that would not normally appear together. — [Geneva dossier](https://www.geneve.ch/actualites/dossiers-information/culture-creatrice-valeurs/culture-innovation/mediation-culturelle-outils-numeriques) (snippet).
- Francke: the object itself speaks, so the "label" is a first-person voice that the player helps save. — [Francke voices page](https://francke-halle.de/en/education/krokoseum/voices) (snippet).
- Obra Dinn: provenance is never stated; the player deduces it and writes it into the ledger. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn).
- Hypnospace Outlaw: pages are framed as archive captures, so each page carries its own "date stamp" logic. — [Filmstories](https://filmstories.co.uk/gaming/hypnospace-outlaw-the-indie-game-that-simulates-the-90s-web/).
- Her Story: the clip's metadata is only the search terms and a watched marker. — [Quarter Disorder](https://quarterdisorder.com/2015/07/09/review-her-story/).

### Inferences
- A site could give each artifact a card with the same fields (title, origin, date, curator's note) and add a "found" field that appears only after discovery (a note, a handbook, a voice line).
- Watched and last-viewed markers (Her Story) are a cheap way to show progress through an archive of documents.

### Gaps
- No museum website was inspected at page level, so label layouts (fonts, placement, field sets) are unverified.
- No verified example of a "provenance" field that is revealed progressively.

## Which mechanics are concrete enough to build: inspecting an object, rotating it, reading a card, unlocking a note?

### Takeaway
Six mechanics are concrete and sourced: object-as-door (Edith Finch, Getty drawers), rotate-and-open (Getty AR), read-a-card-as-document (Objectspace, Researcher SOP), unlock-by-search (Her Story, Hypnospace), unlock-by-quota (Researcher, Francke), and reveal-the-past-by-inspection (Obra Dinn's watch). Each maps to a section or document on a site.

### Cited Findings
- Object-as-door: an object opens its own section (Edith Finch vignettes; Getty drawers open to their text). — sources above.
- Rotate-and-open: a 3D object that the viewer spins, tilts and opens (Getty AR and web interactive). — [Getty AR](https://www.getty.edu/art/exhibitions/north_pavilion/ar/index.html) (snippet).
- Read-a-card: a document with fields, filled by the player (Obra Dinn ledger; Objectspace cards). — sources above.
- Unlock-by-search: sections that are not in navigation and appear only after a search term is found (Hypnospace "Super Search"; Her Story's five-result search). — sources above.
- Unlock-by-quota: a count of collected items unlocks the next note (Researcher, four specimens; Francke's saved voices). — sources above (snippet-level).
- Reveal-by-inspection: a tool that shows the moment an object was last touched (Obra Dinn's pocket watch). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn).

### Inferences
- The least common and most buildable pattern is the combination of a curator's label, a player-filled card, and a discovered note on the same object. Francke and Obra Dinn together cover this.
- For a site where artifacts are sections or documents, the safest first build is object-as-door plus a card, then unlock-by-search for hidden sections (Hypnospace and Her Story show it is feasible in a web interface).
- Per-viewer progress (watched or unlocked) can sit in browser storage. Anything shared between viewers would need a server.

### Gaps
- Mechanics were not verified by playing the games. Object counts and dates are missing for most itch.io and Steam items.
- Fetches of geneve.ch, francke-halle.de, kulturstiftung-des-bundes.de, web.stanford.edu and itch.io pages were blocked, so the claims for those sources rest on search text. Adding these hosts to the session network allowlist would let them be checked in a later pass.
