# Presenting collections as research: field notes, lab notebooks, labels, and "secret lab" framing

Research date: session of 2026-10-29. Most sources below are undated in search results; dates are given only where a URL, title, or snippet shows them. Anything marked (memory) is from memory and unverified.

Method note: this pass used 13 web searches and 0 successful page fetches. The fetches to pcgamer.com, digitaltrends.com and thegamer.com failed because those hosts do not resolve from this session's network allow-list. Every quote below therefore comes from a search-result snippet, not from the full page, unless stated otherwise. Snippet-to-URL mapping is flagged where it is uncertain.

## Key Question 1: Which digital projects present artifacts as specimens, field notes, or case files?

### Takeaway
The clearest real-world case of "specimen tag" presentation is citizen-science label transcription (Zooniverse's Notes from Nature). In museum and library work, the field-notes and label framing shows up mainly as data-entry tasks and as virtual-museum prototypes, not as an in-world "lab" fiction. In games, the notebook or case-file frame is common (Return of the Obra Dinn, Her Story, Papers, Please, Tunic, Subnautica), while a literal "secret lab" fiction is rare outside SCP-branded titles. Rarity ratings below are my inference from what the searches surfaced, not a census.

### Cited Findings

**Citizen science and museum/library**
- Notes from Nature asks volunteers to transcribe specimen labels (insect, plant sheets, fungal labels, bird ledgers). Its FAQ says the project is focused on English-language labels and that non-English labels are transcribed exactly as written, not translated. [Zooniverse Notes from Nature FAQ](https://www.zooniverse.org/projects/md68135/notes-from-nature-ranges-mammal-traits-from-western-north-america/about/faq) (undated; this FAQ is for a sister project, the "Ranges, Mammal Traits" project, so the general label policy is inferred from the same family.)
- A peer-reviewed description in ZooKeys frames transcription as a costly bottleneck in digitizing collections. [ZooKeys 209: 219–233](https://zookeys.pensoft.net/article_preview.php?id=2910) (year not shown in snippet; volume 209 is roughly 2012 (memory, unverified)).
- Milestone: 250,000 transcriptions. [Notes from Nature blog, 2013-08-23](https://blog.notesfromnature.org/2013/08/23/250000-transcriptions/) (date from URL). A Zooniverse daily status post is dated [2014-02-08](https://daily.zooniverse.org/2014/02/08/saturday-status-notes-from-nature) (date from URL; the snippet's ~400,000 figure could not be tied to a specific page).
- Event-based digitization: WeDigBio, a multi-day event where volunteers transcribe specimen information on site and online. [Zooniverse daily, 2016-10-20](https://daily.zooniverse.org/2016/10/20/wedigbio/) (date from URL).
- Virtual museum: an interactive virtual Glasgow University Anatomy Museum built with photogrammetry. Public user tests were positive, and participants said they would consider visiting the real museum. Source: a University of Glasgow eprints record; candidate URLs [eprints.gla.ac.uk/195475](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/195475) or [eprints.gla.ac.uk/220760](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/220760). The snippet-to-URL mapping is unverified. Undated.
- Teaching app: a digitized fetal skull specimen Unity app trialed by 12 pre-medical students at a Glasgow summer school. The authors concluded that more research is needed before such apps can substitute for physical specimens. Likely source: the same eprints domain (URL mapping unverified). Undated.
- Digital specimen networks: the openVertebrate review says significant technological and social hurdles remain before 3D digital specimens reach their potential. Source URL not captured (the snippet came from a search results list); undated.
- Interface metaphor in digital archives: a paper argues that the interface shapes how archives are navigated and understood. [The Interface Metaphor in the Digital Archive (Legrady)](https://www.mat.ucsb.edu/g.legrady/glWeb/publications/p/interface.html) (undated).
- A designer's case study: "Rendered Realities" (Jennifer Wu), a responsive site presenting a collection of 3D artworks pulled from Are.na, argues that interface shapes interpretation. [Jennifer Wu profile (Parsons)](https://www.newschool.edu/parsons/profile/jennifer-wu/) (the project page itself was not captured; this is a designer's self-description, not user research).
- An Anecdoted Archive CD-ROM uses a museum floor plan as its navigation metaphor. The snippet cited it to a paper, but no URL for that detail was captured (undated).

**Games (notebook, case file, specimen-like framing)**
- Return of the Obra Dinn. The player keeps a logbook containing sketches of the crew and an outline of events, and fills in names and fates; clues are stored automatically. [Bit-tech review](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/return-of-the-obra-dinn-review/1/) (undated). PC Gamer describes the notebook as a working tool for cross-referencing objects and names, with the aim of "ruling out possibilities." [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/return-of-the-obra-dinn-review) (date not shown; Obra Dinn released in 2018 (memory, unverified)). A snippet from one review says there is "no hinting, quest markers, or other out-of-context nudging" beyond the book and the memories. [Bit-tech review](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/return-of-the-obra-dinn-review/1/) (undated).
- Her Story: a searchable database of police interview clips. Steam shows 89% positive from 8,394 reviews (snapshot undated; Steam store page URL not captured). One reviewer described the computer interface as making "no sense whatsoever," and another praised the feeling of solving the case through a "trail of breadcrumbs" (quotes from search snippet; no URL captured for them).
- Papers, Please: bureaucratic document-checking. Snippets mention mouse-control complaints and unclear document management in user feedback. Sources: [Honest Gamers review](https://www.honestgamers.com/12056/pc/papers-please/review.html) and [Metacritic user reviews](https://legacy.prod.metacritic.com/game/pc/papers-please/user-reviews) (undated; the "85/100" score in the snippet is unverified).
- Tunic: an in-game instruction manual written partly in an invented language, with pages scattered across the map. [GamesBeat review](https://gamesbeat.com/tunic-review-clever-like-a-fox) and [GameSpot "Best of 2022"](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/tunics-in-game-manual-gives-mechanical-importance-to-collectibles/1100-6509945/) (2022 by the article title; release year from memory). Snippets from [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/tunic-fictional-games-manual-is-a-stroke-of-brilliance/) and [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/reviews/tunic-review/) were seen only in search results; the TheGamer fetch failed.
- SCP: Secret Laboratory (Steam app 700330). A literal "secret lab" title. An aggregator reports about 91% positive across roughly 230,000 reviews (snapshot undated). [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/700330) and [VaporLens stats](https://vaporlens.app/app/700330/scp_secret_laboratory/stats/details). The snippets describe praise for gameplay and team play, and complaints about a toxic community, servers, and optimization. None of the snippets mention its lab fiction.
- A separate Steam listing, "SCP [REDACTED]" (a first-person survival horror game), had a "Mixed" rating from 332 reviews in one snapshot (undated; app ID not confirmed from the snippet).
- Subnautica (scanner and Databank). The Databank is an in-fiction encyclopedia of scanned specimens. Player feedback: [Unknown Worlds forum, "Notes from a new player"](https://forums.unknownworlds.com/discussion/comment/2330668) (undated) and [forum, "Feedback from a newbie who completed 11 runs"](https://forums.unknownworlds.com/discussion/155958/original-subnautica-feedback-from-a-newbie-who-completed-11-runs) (undated).
- Subnautica: Below Zero Databank. [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/848450/discussions/0/4819392984420143897) (undated) calls the Databank a mess; the newly added entries "felt rushed."
- Subnautica 2 Databank: a fan wiki lists 505 PDA entries, with 420 of them containing text and 180 containing illustrations. [wikily.gg Subnautica 2 Databank](https://wikily.gg/subnautica-2/databank/) (fan wiki; not official; undated).
- Furnace in the Archive (itch.io game jam): a commenter says they rarely play text-heavy games. [itch.io comment](https://itch.io/post/12204407/view-in-topic) (URL-to-game mapping unverified; undated).
- A fictional document in a game circulated as real. An AAP fact-check found a "ritual abuse" report that originated from a video game. [AAP FactCheck](https://www.aap.com.au/factcheck/ritual-abuse-report-is-actually-from-video-game/) (undated). This is a risk case: the artifact was convincing enough to be taken as genuine.

### Inferences
- Specimen-label transcription is common in citizen science and well documented (Notes from Nature, WeDigBio). Rarity: common in citizen science; uncommon as a *presented fiction* in museums.
- The notebook or case-file frame is common in indie and narrative games (Obra Dinn, Her Story, Papers, Please, Tunic). Rarity: common.
- A literal "secret lab" or "classified" frame is rare in the sources I found outside SCP-branded titles. Rarity: rare as a presentation device; the SCP name itself is the main example.
- No museum or library digital project in my results used a fictional "field notes" or "classified file" layer. Their notes-and-labels framing was documentary (transcription, provenance, and virtual replicas). Rarity: rare.

### Gaps
- The searches did not surface a museum or library project that frames its objects as a secret or classified research program. I searched for named projects such as "Anatomy of the Unknown," "Tissue Map," and "Sea Dragon." None turned up, so I cannot confirm they exist.
- Fetches failed for pcgamer.com, digitaltrends.com, and thegamer.com. A network allow-list would have to permit these hosts before the full texts can be checked.
- No Reddit or Hacker News thread was retrieved. A search result pointed to an r/ObraDinn listing via a third-party mirror (r.datuan.dev), which I did not open.

## Key Question 2: What presentation devices work?

### Takeaway
The devices with the most support in the sources are: notebooks or logbooks that record the player's own deductions (Obra Dinn), scattered in-world documents that carry essential rules (Tunic, with mixed results), and searchable records (Her Story). I found no user evidence for stamped records or timelines of discovery specifically. Marginalia and handwriting were mentioned by designers and reviewers but not tested.

### Cited Findings
- Notebook as the central tool: sketches of the crew and an outline of events, with clues stored automatically. [Bit-tech review](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/return-of-the-obra-dinn-review/1/) (undated).
- Notebook as a tool for elimination: "ruling out possibilities." [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/return-of-the-obra-dinn-review) (date not shown; text from snippet).
- Pages as puzzle and collectible: "one review calls the manual its own puzzle." [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/tunic-review-clever-like-a-fox) and [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/tunics-in-game-manual-gives-mechanical-importance-to-collectibles/1100-6509945/) (2022).
- Searchable records as a discovery method: Her Story reviewers praised the "trail of breadcrumbs." (No URL captured for this quote.)
- Virtual specimens as teaching: the Glasgow virtual museum and the fetal skull app (undated, mapping unverified).
- Interface affects interpretation (designer claim): [Rendered Realities / Jennifer Wu](https://www.newschool.edu/parsons/profile/jennifer-wu/) (designer self-description).

### Inferences
- Devices that record the player's own reasoning (notebooks, elimination grids) seem to be the most praised, because the player's effort becomes the artifact. This rests on reviewer praise only.
- Devices that hide required information in optional documents seem to create friction (see KQ3 and KQ4).

### Gaps
- No A/B or user-study evidence was found comparing handwritten vs. typed labels, stamps, marginalia, or timelines. These claims remain untested in what I could access.
- The Glasgow virtual museum evaluation is a small public user test. Its sample size and method were not retrieved.

## Key Question 3: How do users respond?

### Takeaway
Player responses are mixed and mostly come from reviews and forum threads, not from formal user research. Players praise deduction-by-document games (Obra Dinn, Her Story) and report satisfaction at solving them. Common complaints are overload at the start, unclear interface decisions, and information hidden in optional pages or entries. I found no formal user study of the notebook or case-file presentation itself.

### Cited Findings

**Positive / engaged**
- Obra Dinn: PC Gamer calls it "one of the best puzzle games on PC" and says it shows "real confidence in the player." [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/return-of-the-obra-dinn-review) (date not shown).
- Obra Dinn user reviews (via a Metacritic mirror, metacriticz.pages.dev, which is unofficial): "I loved the satisfaction of slowly solving the ship's mysteries." and "I became addicted to getting my answers correct." [Mirror of Metacritic user reviews](https://metacriticz.pages.dev/game/return-of-the-obra-dinn/user-reviews/) (undated; unofficial mirror).
- Her Story: a Steam reviewer praised feeling like they were solving the case themselves. (Steam, 89% positive; snapshot undated.)
- Tunic: reviewers praise the manual as a homage to old Nintendo-style manuals, and say page discovery can happen in nearly every session. [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/tunic-review-clever-like-a-fox) (2022 by context).
- Glasgow virtual museum: public user tests were positive, and participants said they would consider visiting the real museum. [eprints.gla.ac.uk](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/195475) (URL mapping unverified; undated).

**Negative / confused / overwhelmed**
- Obra Dinn: one reviewer said they were "very overwhelmed" when they started playing. Source: Digital Trends (snippet only; fetch failed). [Digital Trends impressions](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/return-of-the-obra-dinn-impressions-review) (date not shown).
- Obra Dinn: some reviewers say the notebook stage is where the game "kind of drops off." Source URL not captured for this quote.
- Obra Dinn: navigating multiple menus and the book with a controller was described as "a bit cumbersome." Source: a review in the search results (URL not captured).
- Obra Dinn: some fate explanations make it hard to pin down how someone died. [GodisaGeek review](https://www.godisageek.com/reviews/return-of-the-obra-dinn-review/) (undated).
- Obra Dinn: a blogger calls the game's "annoying UX choices" its main flaw. [jmduke.com](https://www.jmduke.com/posts/return-of-the-obra-dinn.md) (undated).
- Her Story: one reviewer found the computer interface "makes no sense whatsoever." (Steam; no URL captured.)
- Papers, Please: user feedback mentions mouse-control difficulties and unclear document management. [Honest Gamers](https://www.honestgamers.com/12056/pc/papers-please/review.html) (undated).
- Tunic: some players missed pages or reached key solutions through aimless backtracking or luck; critics say leveling and consumable information is hidden inside collectible pages, which can alienate players. [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/tunic-review-clever-like-a-fox) and [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/tunics-in-game-manual-gives-mechanical-importance-to-collectibles/1100-6509945/) (2022 by title; snippet-based).
- Subnautica: a new player said the scanner is not highlighted as central to the gameplay loop. [Unknown Worlds forum](https://forums.unknownworlds.com/discussion/comment/2330668) (undated).
- Subnautica: an 11-run player noted that an unlocked blueprint has no matching Databank entry. [Unknown Worlds forum](https://forums.unknownworlds.com/discussion/155958/original-subnautica-feedback-from-a-newbie-who-completed-11-runs) (undated).
- Subnautica: Below Zero Databank entries lagged behind the game during early access, and "veterans stop reading them." [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/848450/discussions/0/4819392984420143897) (undated).
- Furnace in the Archive: a commenter who rarely plays text-heavy games found the reading-based design sympathetic; a separate reviewer found it hard to tell what to redact versus archive. [itch.io comment](https://itch.io/post/12204407/view-in-topic) (mapping unverified; undated).

### Inferences
- Engagement appears highest when the player's own deduction is the product (Obra Dinn). Confusion appears when the interface asks players to find essential rules or data inside optional material (Tunic, Subnautica Databank).
- Reactions are reported by reviewers and forum users. Their self-selection (people who write reviews or forum posts) limits what can be generalized.

### Gaps
- No formal user research (interviews, usability tests, or eye-tracking) was found for any of these notebook, case-file, or specimen-label interfaces, outside the small Glasgow virtual museum test.
- Reddit and Hacker News threads on these interfaces were not retrieved.
- The Her Story Steam review quotes and the Papers, Please Metacritic score could not be tied to specific, fetched URLs.

## Key Question 4: Where does "secret lab" framing tip into gimmick, and what do critics say?

### Takeaway
The clearest critical signal is not about the fiction itself but about whether the frame holds information players need. Critics and players single out essential rules hidden in optional documents (Tunic), descriptive entries that lag or are skipped (Subnautica Databank), and interfaces that obscure what the player should do (Obra Dinn, Her Story). I found no critic who explicitly calls a "secret lab" or "classified" frame a gimmick. The phrase "gimmick" does not appear in any source I retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Tunic: a critic says that information on leveling and consumables is hidden inside collectible pages, which "risks alienating some players." [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/tunic-review-clever-like-a-fox) and [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/tunics-in-game-manual-gives-mechanical-importance-to-collectibles/1100-6509945/) (snippet-based).
- Subnautica: Databank entries lag behind the game, and veterans stop reading them. [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/848450/discussions/0/4819392984420143897) (undated).
- Obra Dinn: "annoying UX choices" described as the main flaw by one blogger. [jmduke.com](https://www.jmduke.com/posts/return-of-the-obra-dinn.md) (undated).
- A fictional document in a game circulated as real. [AAP FactCheck](https://www.aap.com.au/factcheck/ritual-abuse-report-is-actually-from-video-game/) (undated). This is a distinct risk: the artifact works too well as a document, and the fiction can escape the game.
- Designer-level critique: a commenter in a found-documents jam page says the reading-based design is hard to sort, so the frame sometimes asks for work the player did not expect. [itch.io comment](https://itch.io/post/12204407/view-in-topic) (mapping unverified).

### Inferences
- A presentation tips into gimmick when it (a) hides information needed to progress, (b) makes the artifact harder to read than the content it carries, or (c) asks for busywork (searching, backtracking) that the fiction does not reward. The sources support (a) and (c) most clearly.
- SCP: Secret Laboratory shows that a "secret lab" name can coexist with strong reviews when reviewers talk about gameplay and social play, not the lab fiction. This suggests the framing is not what most reviewers evaluate. This is an inference from snippets, not a direct finding.

### Gaps
- No formal design critique of the "secret lab" or "classified file" frame was found. Searches for "gimmick," "too much reading," and "fake archive" returned mostly literary and forgery material, not game criticism.
- Critics' views on Immortality, Telling Lies, and similar FMV or document-based titles were not searched.
- The critical write-ups that might address the gimmick question (for example, GDC talks or postmortems about Obra Dinn's interface) were not retrieved.
